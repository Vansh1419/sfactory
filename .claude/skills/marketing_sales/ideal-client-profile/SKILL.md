# Ideal client profile

**Status: draft v0.1.** The founder has not written the final profile yet. This is a starting point built from the charter. When the founder gives a different rule in a task, the task wins. Do not make up facts about the company to fill gaps.

## Who we sell to

The company sells four services to customers worldwide: **web development, AI automation, marketing and content.** A good lead is a business or person who:

1. Needs at least one of those four services, and says so or clearly shows it (for example, an old or broken website, a slow manual process, no content for their product).
2. Can decide and pay: a small or mid-sized business, a startup, an agency or a solo professional. Not a student project and not a very large company with a long buying process.
3. Has a real, reachable business contact: a public business email or a contact page. Never a private person's personal details found by digging.

## How to judge a lead

Give each lead one of these, with one line of reasons:

- **Good fit:** needs one of our services, can pay, and has a business contact.
- **Maybe:** one of the three is unclear. Say which one.
- **Not a fit:** none of our services are needed, or there is no way to reach them properly.

Only write a proposal for a **Good fit** lead, or when the task tells you to.

## What to write down about a lead

Keep the notes short and factual. Each fact comes from a page you read or a search result; say where it came from. Include:

- what the business does, in one sentence
- which of our four services they seem to need, and what you saw that shows it
- the business email or contact page you found
- the fit rating and your reason

Do **not** write down: personal social media, home addresses, phone numbers of private people, guesses about money or health, or anything you could not find on a public page. If you are not sure, leave it out.
