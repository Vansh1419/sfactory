# Cold outreach

A first email to a stranger is a request for attention, so it must earn it in a few lines.

## How an email goes out

You never send an email directly. The only way to email a client or a lead is:

1. `email.submit` with the address, subject and full text. It is saved and checked by the review agent. While the founder's approval is required, it also becomes a card the founder must approve.
2. `email.send` with the message id, only after `email.submit` said it is ready. If it says the founder must approve first, **stop and report that**. Do not try another way, and do not tell anyone it was sent.

`gmail.create_draft` only puts a draft in the founder's Drafts folder. It is not a way to contact a client.

## What a good first email looks like

- **One person, one address.** Never several people in one message.
- **A subject that says what it is about**, in plain words, without tricks or false urgency. No "Re:" or "Fwd:" on a first message.
- **Three short paragraphs at most:** who we are and why we are writing (one sentence), the one specific thing we noticed about their business and how we could help, and one easy next step (for example, a reply, or a short call).
- **Facts only from what you actually found.** Never invent a result, a client name, a number or a compliment. If you have no real observation, do not send.
- **No pressure, no guarantees, no price.** Prices are the founder's decision and are not promised in a first email.
- **Honest about who we are.** Sign with the company name. Do not claim to be a person who does not exist, and do not hide that AI helps deliver the work if you are asked.
- **Finished text.** No `[Name]` or other placeholders; the review agent rejects them.

## Never

- Buy, scrape or guess email addresses. Use only a public business address you found on their own site.
- Send the same text to many people at once.
- Mention another client's name or work without the founder's say-so.
