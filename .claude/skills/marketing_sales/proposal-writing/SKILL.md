# Proposal writing

A proposal turns what we learned about a lead into a clear offer. You **draft** it; the founder decides whether it goes out.

## What a proposal contains

1. **Their situation**, in two or three sentences, using only facts from the lead's notes. Say where something is a guess.
2. **What we would do**, in plain words, tied to one or more of our four services: web development, AI automation, marketing, content. Name the work, not the tools.
3. **What they get**, as a short list of results they can check.
4. **How it would run**, in a few steps, with rough timing in weeks. Say it is a rough estimate.
5. **The price.** Only write a price if the task gives you one, and write it exactly as given, with the currency. If the task gives none, write: "We will send you a price after a short call." **Never make up, guess or round a price.** Setting or changing a price is the founder's decision.
6. **The next step:** one simple thing the client can do.

## Rules

- **Never promise** results, dates or prices the task did not give you. No "guaranteed", no "best in the world".
- **Never commit the company** to a contract, a signature, a discount or a refund. We are one person's business: anything signed is the founder's personal commitment.
- **Be honest about AI.** If asked, say AI agents help deliver the work and the founder oversees it.
- **No placeholders.** The text is finished; the review agent rejects `[Name]` and the like.
- **No secrets, no other clients' details.**

## How it goes to the founder

1. Save the full proposal as a file with `drive.save_file` when Drive is available, or hand the text back in your result.
2. Send the email that carries it with `email.submit` (see the cold-outreach skill). That puts it through review and, while required, onto the founder's approval card.
3. End your result with: the lead's name, the message id, and whether it is **ready** or **waiting for the founder**. Say clearly that nothing has been sent to the client yet unless `email.send` confirmed it.
