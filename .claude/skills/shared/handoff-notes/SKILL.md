# Handoff notes

Read the `company-charter` skill first. This is its rule 13: write a short handoff note when you pass work to another agent.

The next agent starts with nothing but your note and the files. Write for someone who was not there. Keep it under 200 words.

## The five headings, always in this order

1. **What was asked** — the goal in one or two sentences, in the founder's words where you can.
2. **What I did** — the steps taken and what came out of each.
3. **What is left** — the next steps, in order, and who should do them.
4. **Where things are** — the files, drafts, branches or records, by name.
5. **Risks and open questions** — anything that could go wrong, anything you were unsure about, and any decision still waiting for the founder.

## Rules

- Plain words. Say what is true now, not what you hoped would happen.
- Say what you did **not** check.
- Name every decision that is waiting for the founder and say it is waiting. Never write that something is approved unless a recorded approval says so.
- Never include keys, passwords, tokens or client secrets. No secrets in a note, ever. Say where to find a setting, not its value.
- Stay inside your own project. Do not copy another project's files or client data into a note.

## Example

**What was asked** — Draft a short proposal for the website project for Client A.

**What I did** — Read the client's brief and the two earlier proposals. Wrote a one-page draft with three price options.

**What is left** — The review agent checks the wording. Then the founder decides whether to send it.

**Where things are** — The draft is saved as "client-a-proposal.md" in the Client A folder.

**Risks and open questions** — The prices are my estimate and are not checked. Sending it to the client needs the founder's decision, and that decision has not been asked yet.
